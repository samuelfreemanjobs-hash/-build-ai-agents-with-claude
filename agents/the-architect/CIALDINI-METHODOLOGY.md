# Robert Cialdini Methodology

Persuasion psychology for The Architect. **One voice** — Cialdini supplies the science of *why* people say yes, how to ethically channel attention before the ask, and how to engineer belief through the seven principles. Not a separate mode.

**Core texts:** *Influence: The Psychology of Persuasion* (New and Expanded), *Pre-Suasion: A Revolutionary Way to Influence and Persuade*, *Influence at Work* research

---

## Cialdini in One Sentence

> **People say yes to those they owe, those they've committed to, those like them, those with proof, those with authority, those they like, those with scarce access, and those who share their identity — so earn the yes ethically by giving first, pre-suading attention, and stacking principles where they genuinely serve the reader.**

---

## Philosophical Foundation: Ethical Influence

Cialdini distinguishes **influence** (mutual benefit, transparent, reader wins) from **manipulation** (exploitation, hidden motive, reader loses).

| Manipulation (reject) | Ethical influence (Architect) |
|---|---|
| Fake scarcity, fabricated proof | True deadlines, real numbers, verifiable claims |
| Weaponizing vulnerability | Serving the starving crowd with genuine relief |
| Trick closes, bait-and-switch | Risk reversal + honest prescription (Abraham) |
| Pressure without value | Reciprocity first — Results in Advance (Kern) |

**Architect rule:** Every principle must pass the *"Would I want this used on me by someone I trust?"* test. If the offer doesn't help, don't write the copy.

## The Seven Principles of Influence

Each principle is a **psychological shortcut** humans use under uncertainty and overload. Copy should activate the right lever at the right moment — never carpet-bomb all seven.

### 1. Reciprocity

**Mechanism:** We feel obligated to return favors — especially unexpected, personalized, meaningful gifts.

| Trigger | Copy / ad application |
|---|---|
| Give first | Lead magnets, free chapters, audits, samples, Hopkins-style trials |
| Unexpected value | Bonus not advertised upfront; "I wasn't going to include this, but…" |
| Personalized | Segment-specific resource, not generic PDF |
| No strings attached | Deliver value before any pitch; Kern Results in Advance |

**Copy moves:**
- Open with insight or tool, not logo
- "Before I ask anything, here's the [checklist/template/audit]…"
- Content marketing that solves one acute problem completely

**Watch out:** Obligation fades if the "gift" feels like a disguised ad. Give real value.

---

### 2. Commitment & Consistency

**Mechanism:** Once we take a small stand — especially publicly, actively, voluntarily — we pressure ourselves to stay consistent with it.

| Trigger | Copy / ad application |
|---|---|
| Foot-in-the-door | Micro-yes before macro-yes: quiz, checklist, opt-in, webinar RSVP |
| Active commitment | Type, click, write — not passive scroll |
| Public / social | Share wins, community posts, cohort visibility |
| Low-ball (ethical) | Start with honest entry offer; ascension feels like natural next step |
| Labeling | "You're the kind of person who…" (only when true to their identity) |

**Copy moves:**
- "Answer these 3 questions" before the pitch
- "If you've read this far, you're not like most [avatar] who quit"
- Application funnels: short form → longer form → call
- Written goals, worksheets, implementation pledges

**Watch out:** Forcing false identity labels backfires. Consistency must align with who they want to become (Kern Core Identity).

---

### 3. Social Proof

**Mechanism:** Under uncertainty, we look to others — especially **similar others** — to decide what's correct.

| Trigger | Copy / ad application |
|---|---|
| Numbers | "12,847 members," "Used in 43 countries" |
| Similar others | Testimonials from people like the reader (age, situation, industry) |
| Uncertainty | Social proof matters most when the decision feels risky or novel |
| Dynamic proof | "147 people enrolled this week" (only if true) |
| Expert crowd | "Trusted by [peers they respect]" |

**Copy moves:**
- Testimonial wall matched to avatar segments
- Case studies with specific before/after (Hopkins specificity)
- "Most popular" tier labeling
- User-generated results, screenshots, video proof
- "Join [name], [name], and [name] who already…"

**Watch out:** Fake testimonials, stock photos, inflated numbers = manipulation + legal risk. Use real proof or none.

---

### 4. Authority

**Mechanism:** We defer to credible experts — credentials, experience, symbols of expertise.

| Trigger | Copy / ad application |
|---|---|
| Credentials | Degrees, certifications, years, books published |
| Demonstrated expertise | Show don't tell: mechanism, process, case depth |
| Authority symbols | "As featured in," logos, awards (real only) |
| Preemptive honesty | Admit a weakness to boost trust on strengths (Hopkins preemptive truth) |
| Trust transfer | Host-beneficiary endorsement (Abraham) |

**Copy moves:**
- Bio block with relevant credentials — not vanity list
- "Here's exactly how we get this result" (reason-why)
- Third-party validation near the claim it supports
- Confident guarantee as authority signal (risk reversal)

**Watch out:** Empty titles and logo walls without substance. Authority must connect to the claim.

---

### 5. Liking

**Mechanism:** We say yes more easily to people we like — similarity, compliments, cooperation, association.

| Trigger | Copy / ad application |
|---|---|
| Similarity | Shared struggles, vocabulary, background ("I was where you are") |
| Compliments | Genuine praise of reader's intelligence, taste, effort |
| Cooperation | "We're on the same side against [common enemy]" |
| Association | Positive context, attractive presentation, likable messenger |
| Peer tone | Kern conversational — not corporate pitch |

**Copy moves:**
- "If you're a [avatar] who [specific behavior], you already know…"
- Enemy alignment: bad advice, broken industry, past failures (not the reader)
- Founder story that mirrors reader pain
- Warm, human voice — Halbert A-Pile personal

**Watch out:** Flattery that insults intelligence. Liking comes from respect, not slime.

---

### 6. Scarcity

**Mechanism:** We want more of what's less available — especially if **newly** scarce or lost.

| Trigger | Copy / ad application |
|---|---|
| Limited quantity | Seats, spots, inventory caps |
| Limited time | Real deadlines, cohort start dates |
| Exclusive access | "Not sold anywhere else" |
| Loss framing | What they lose by waiting — not only what they gain |
| Newly scarce | "Doors closing Friday" beats vague "limited" |

**Copy moves:**
- Specific numbers: "12 spots left" not "limited spots"
- Deadline with reason (cohort, capacity, bonus expiry)
- Kennedy/Abraham ethical urgency — true or don't use
- Fear of missing transformation, not fake countdown timers

**Watch out:** Fabricated scarcity destroys trust permanently. Cialdini + Architect ethics: **true or cut**.

---

### 7. Unity

**Mechanism:** We say yes to those who share **identity** — tribe, values, "we" — beyond mere similarity.

| Trigger | Copy / ad application |
|---|---|
| Shared identity | "As founders, we…" "Fellow [tribe]…" |
| Co-creation | Community input, member naming, shared mission |
| Kinship language | We/us vs they/them (ethical, not divisive manipulation) |
| Values alignment | What we stand for — and against |
| Core Identity | Kern + Cialdini unity = who they're becoming |

**Copy moves:**
- "Welcome to the [tribe name] who refuse to [common failure]"
- Insider vocabulary from VOC research
- Manifesto-style positioning: this is who we are
- Community proof: "people like us do things like this"

**Watch out:** Artificial tribalism, us-vs-them cruelty, exclusion for ego. Unity serves belonging, not hate.

## Pre-Suasion: Channel Attention Before the Message

From *Pre-Suasion* — the **privileged moment** before the main pitch, when attention can be directed toward concepts associated with your goal.

### Core insight

> **What's focal is causal.** What people attend to first seems more important, more true, more relevant.

### Pre-suasion in copy & ads

| Technique | Application |
|---|---|
| **Openers that prime** | Headline/lead channels attention to problem, identity, or value before the offer |
| **Question priming** | "How honest are you?" before honesty-related pitch |
| **Context setting** | Story opening that makes the mechanism feel inevitable |
| **Visual / environmental** | Hero image, background, color — for ads and landing pages (note in brief) |
| **Privileged moment** | Email PS, webinar first 5 minutes, above-the-fold — highest attention real estate |
| **Chutes not ladders** | Single path of attention — don't scatter focus before CTA |

### Pre-suasion checklist (before the ask)

1. What concept must be **focal** when they hit the CTA? (problem severity? authority? community?)
2. What do they see/read **first**? Does it prime that concept?
3. Are you asking for action while attention is on **benefit** or **cost**?
4. Did you waste the privileged moment on logistics, "about us," or weak filler?

### Copy sequence (pre-suade → persuade)

```
ATTENTION CHANNEL (pre-suasion)
    ↓
CONCEPT PRIMING (problem / identity / proof / scarcity)
    ↓
MESSAGE (PAS, story, mechanism)
    ↓
PRINCIPLE STACK (reciprocity already given → social proof → authority → scarcity at close)
    ↓
CTA (commitment device)
```

## The Unity of 30,000 Feet: Principle Stacking

Don't use one principle per ad. **Layer** them across the asset and funnel:

| Funnel stage | Primary principles |
|---|---|
| **Top** | Reciprocity (value), Liking (voice), Unity (tribe), Pre-suasion (hook) |
| **Middle** | Social proof, Authority, Commitment (micro-yes) |
| **Bottom** | Scarcity (if true), Consistency (identity close), Reciprocity (bonus reveal) |

### Example: sales page principle map

| Section | Principles active |
|---|---|
| Headline / lead | Pre-suasion, Liking, Unity |
| Problem (PAS) | Pre-suasion, Commitment (self-identification) |
| Mechanism | Authority, Consistency (logic) |
| Proof block | Social proof, Authority |
| Offer stack | Reciprocity (bonuses), Scarcity (if true) |
| Guarantee | Authority, Reciprocity (risk on us) |
| CTA | Commitment, Scarcity, Consistency ("you're ready") |

## Writing Ads: Cialdini Lens

### Short-form ads (T1–T2)

| Ad type | Lead principle | Structure |
|---|---|---|
| **Lead gen** | Reciprocity | Specific free outcome + curiosity gap |
| **Retargeting** | Consistency | "You looked at X — here's the next step" |
| **Cold traffic** | Social proof or Unity | "Join 8,000 [avatar]" / "Built for [tribe]" |
| **Urgency ad** | Scarcity (true) | Deadline + loss frame |
| **Founder ad** | Liking + Authority | Story + credential in 2 lines |

### Long-form ads / advertorials

1. Pre-suade with story or stat (channel attention to problem)
2. PAS body (Kennedy) with social proof embedded
3. Authority demonstration (mechanism)
4. Offer with reciprocity stack
5. Close with scarcity + commitment CTA

## Key Cialdini Lines (operational)

| Line | Use when |
|---|---|
| *"People say yes to those they owe"* | Weak lead magnets, pitch-first copy |
| *"What's focal is causal"* | Weak openings, buried leads |
| *"Similar others under uncertainty"* | Missing or mismatched testimonials |
| *"The request should follow the gift"* | Ask before value |
| *"Active, public, voluntary commitments"* | No engagement steps in funnel |
| *"Exclusive information and access"* | Generic offers with no real scarcity |
| *"We, not they"* | Copy that sounds corporate, not tribal |
| *"Pre-suade before you persuade"* | CTA feels abrupt or unearned |
