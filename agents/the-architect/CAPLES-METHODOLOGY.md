# John Caples Methodology

Tested advertising methods for The Architect. **One voice** — Caples supplies headline science, story-driven hooks, before/after structure, and the discipline of split-run testing. Not a separate mode.

**Core texts:** *Tested Advertising Methods*, *How to Make Your Advertising Make Money*, BBDO-era split-run research

---

## Caples in One Sentence

> **The headline is the ad — test it first, appeal to self-interest, tell a story that selects the right reader, prove every claim with specifics, and let long copy sell when you've earned attention.**

---

## Philosophical Foundation: Tested, Not Clever

Caples and Hopkins share a lineage: advertising is **measured salesmanship**, not art for awards.

| Clever advertising (reject) | Tested advertising (Caples) |
|---|---|
| Headlines that entertain but don't select buyers | Headlines that pick out the prospect you can help |
| Humor, puns, blind curiosity | Self-interest, news, story, specificity |
| Short copy by default | Long copy when the reader is interested — *"The more you tell, the more you sell"* |
| Guessing what works | Split-run tests; headline tested first |
| Company name in headline | Reader benefit in headline |

**Architect rule:** Every headline gets variants. Every ad has a test hypothesis. Opinion < measured response.

---

## The Caples Law: Headline = 80% of the Ad

Caples proved that changing the headline alone could multiply response **5–10×** with identical body copy.

| Implication | Action |
|---|---|
| Test headlines first | Split-run before rewriting body |
| Headline selects the reader | Like calling *"Hey, Bill Jones"* in a crowd (Hopkins) |
| Body can't rescue weak headline | Fix T1 before writing T3 |
| 10 variants minimum | Different appeal type per variant when relevant |

**Workflow:** Write 10 headline variants → classify by Caples type → pick 3 to test → body follows winning angle.

## Five Ways to Get Attention (Headline Appeals)

| Appeal | Mechanism | Example pattern |
|---|---|---|
| **Self-interest** | What's in it for me? | "How to win friends and influence people" |
| **News** | New, now, announcing, at last | "New discovery…" "Now you can…" |
| **Curiosity** | Open loop — must read on | "They laughed when… but when…" |
| **Quick & easy way** | Shortcut to desired outcome | "Play a simple tune in 7 days" |
| **Story** | Narrative pulls reader in | "They grinned when the waiter spoke to me in French…" |

**Combine appeals** when possible: news + self-interest + curiosity = strongest hooks.

## Caples Headline Types (write variants across types)

| Type | Structure | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| **How-to** | How to [desired outcome] | Solution-aware, instructional offers |
| **Question** | Do you make these mistakes in [topic]? | Problem-aware; implicates reader |
| **News** | New / Now / Announcing / At last | New mechanism, product, offer |
| **Reason-why** | Why [surprising claim] | Mechanism curiosity |
| **Testimonial / story** | They laughed when… / I used to… | Emotional proof, identification |
| **Command** | Stop [pain] / Start [gain] | Urgent, direct offers |
| **Before/after** | From [bad state] to [good state] | Transformation offers |
| **Specific number** | [Number] ways / in [timeframe] | Credibility, concreteness |
| **Quiz / checklist** | Which of these… / Score yourself | Engagement, self-diagnosis |

### Classic Caples formulas (extract structure, never plagiarize)

| Formula | Structural move |
|---|---|
| *They laughed when I [action] — but when I [result]…* | Ridicule → reversal → payoff tease |
| *Do you make these mistakes in [topic]?* | Implicate reader in a list they fear |
| *How to [benefit] — [ease/time qualifier]* | Self-interest + quick way |
| *Which of these [items] do you want most?* | Quiz engagement + choice |
| *Here is a [method] that is helping [avatar] to [outcome]* | News + social proof seed |
| *The [adjective] way to [benefit]* | Positioning + self-interest |

## Headline Rules (Caples checklist)

- [ ] **Selects the right reader** — wrong people self-filter out
- [ ] **Self-interest obvious** — benefit visible in headline
- [ ] **News angle** when truthful (new, now, discovery, method)
- [ ] **Curiosity** — gap that demands resolution
- [ ] **Specific** — numbers, timeframes, concrete outcomes
- [ ] **Not tricky** — no puns, no blind headlines, no "guess what we sell"
- [ ] **Company name out** — unless the company IS the news (rare)
- [ ] **Negative can work** — "Don't" / mistakes / warnings often outpull positive
- [ ] **Long headline OK** — if every word sells; cut filler only
- [ ] **Visual pairing noted** — headline + image must tell one story (for ads)

## Body Copy: Caples Laws

### 1. Long copy outsells short — when interest is earned

If the headline selected the right reader, **give the full story**. Interested prospects want information. Caples and Hopkins agree: brief ads fail keyed tests.

| Situation | Length |
|---|---|
| Cold, low awareness | Shorter lead; earn attention fast |
| Problem/solution aware | Long-form OK — prove and stack |
| High-ticket / high consideration | Long copy + proof parade |

### 2. Interest = self-interest on every line

Ask continuously: *"Why should they read the next sentence?"*  
Each paragraph must advance **their** outcome — not your company history.

### 3. Be specific (Caples + Hopkins)

| Weak | Caples-strong |
|---|---|
| Save money on groceries | Cut your food bill 47% |
| Learn piano fast | Play a simple tune in 7 days |
| Better skin | Clearer skin in 17 days — or money back |

### 4. Prove every claim

- Testimonials with full name and context when possible
- Demonstrations, before/after, figures
- Reason-why for mechanism claims
- **Pictures that sell** — before/after, product in use, story illustration; remove decorative images

### 5. Make it easy to read

- Short paragraphs (1–4 lines)
- Subheads every 3–5 paragraphs
- Underlining, bullets, numbered lists for scanners
- **Slippery slide** — end paragraphs pulling into next (Halbert)
- Read-aloud test — tongue twisters fail

### 6. Tell the complete story

One reading = one decision. Don't assume they saw your last ad. New prospect only.

### 7. Offer a service

Free booklet, sample, trial, audit, demonstration — reader asks for it; psychology shifts (Hopkins sampling).

## Before/After (Caples signature structure)

**Use for:** transformations, fitness, finance, skills, business results.

| Element | Job |
|---|---|
| **Before** | Specific pain state — reader sees themselves |
| **Turning point** | Discovery, method, decision |
| **After** | Specific improved state — numbers, scenes |
| **Mechanism** | Why it worked — reason-why |
| **CTA** | How they get the same result |

**Visual before/after** doubles power in display ads — note image direction in brief.

## Story Headlines & Story Leads

Caples' most famous ads are **story headlines** — mini-narratives that create identification before the pitch.

**Story lead sequence:**
1. Scene (specific moment)
2. Conflict or ridicule
3. Turn ("but when…")
4. Payoff tease (open loop)
5. Bridge to offer

**Voice:** Same Architect cinematic tone — Caples story structure, not melodrama.

## Illustrations & Display Ads (Caples)

| Rule | Application |
|---|---|
| Picture must **sell** | Product in use, before/after, demonstration — not decoration |
| Headline + image = one story | Mismatch kills response |
| Captions count | Captions read 2× body — write them |
| Human faces & action | Stop the scroll; create identification |
| Remove non-selling art | Pretty ≠ profitable |

For digital: note hero image, thumbnail, and video still requirements in delivery.

## Testing Discipline (Caples + Hopkins)

| Rule | Practice |
|---|---|
| **Test headlines first** | Biggest lever; cheapest test |
| **One variable at a time** | Headline A vs B — same body |
| **Keyed response** | Coupon, code, UTM, pixel — measure cost per sale |
| **Volume before verdict** | Statistical significance; don't kill winners early |
| **Winner = new control** | Then test body, offer, image |
| **Record everything** | Swipe winners to `SWIPE-FILE.md` with metrics |

**Caples truth:** *"Almost any question can be answered by a test campaign."*

## Common Mistakes (Caples)

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Clever headline | Clear self-interest headline |
| Company name in headline | Benefit to reader |
| Humor | Sell — don't entertain |
| Blind headline | Reader knows what's offered |
| Short copy by reflex | Long copy when headline earned interest |
| Weak first sentence | Story or problem — never "We are pleased to announce" |
| No subheads | Scanner loses the thread |
| Unproven superlatives | Specifics + proof |
| Pretty pictures | Selling pictures |
