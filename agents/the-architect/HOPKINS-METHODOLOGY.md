# Claude Hopkins Methodology

Scientific Advertising + My Life in Advertising — synthesized for The Architect.  
**One voice.** Hopkins is the grandfather of measurable persuasion: salesmanship in print, reason-why copy, specificity, testing. Voice stays The Architect; Hopkins supplies discipline and craft laws.

**Primary source:** *Scientific Advertising* (1923) — public domain. Hosted at [thegaryhalbertletter.com](https://thegaryhalbertletter.com/Products_&_Affiliates/ScientificAdvertisingUnMarked.html)

---

## Hopkins in One Sentence

> **Advertising is salesmanship multiplied. Its only purpose is to make sales. Every claim must be specific, every ad must be testable, and the product should do the selling whenever possible.**

---

## The Hopkins Laws (non-negotiable)

### 1. Advertising = Salesmanship
- Not entertainment, not "brand awareness," not applause for clever copy
- Ask: *"Would this help a salesman sell face-to-face?"*
- Fine writing, literary style, slogans, and clever conceits are **disadvantages** — they reveal the hook
- Mediocre ads affect **all** your trade; mediocre salesmen affect only part

### 2. Scientific Method
- Advertising laws are established by **tested results**, not opinions
- Keyed tracking (coupons, codes, UTMs, pixels) — without measurement, it's gambling
- Compare one variable at a time; winner becomes new control
- Mail-order discipline is the model: *"The more you tell, the more you sell"* (when the reader is interested)

### 3. Reason-Why Copy
- Don't say "best" — say **why** it's best
- Give logical, verifiable reasons the reader can evaluate
- **Preemptive truth (Schlitz):** State what everyone does but no one says — first to tell owns the claim
- Mechanism = Hopkins reason-why (decades before the word existed)

### 4. Be Specific
> *"Platitudes roll off the human understanding like water from a duck."*

| Weak (platitude) | Strong (Hopkins) |
|---|---|
| Best in the world | 1,018 experiments to perfect the yeast |
| Lowest prices | Our net profit is 3% |
| Quick shave | 78-second shave |
| Abundant lather | Softens beard in one minute; creamy ten minutes |
| Pure beer | Washed four times; water from 4,000 feet deep |

**Rule:** A specific claim is believed or disbelieved — vague claims are ignored.

### 5. Offer Service (Not "Buy My Brand")
- People are selfish — they care about **their** problem, not your profit
- Best ads ask no one to buy directly; they offer **service**: information, samples, trials
- Picture the customer's side until buying is the natural result
- *"I was sent to give you a brush — take your choice"* beats *"Buy my brushes"*

### 6. Headlines Select the Audience
- Purpose: pick out people you **can** interest — like calling *"Hey, Bill Jones"* in a crowd
- Blind headlines and clever conceits attract wrong readers
- Hopkins spent **hours** on headlines; scored discarded; keyed returns proved **5–10×** swings from headline alone
- Address only the people you seek

### 7. Tell the Full Story
- One reading = one decision. Present **every** important claim in that ad
- Serial ads don't connect. Brief untraced ads fail.
- Write for **new customers only** — current users won't read your ads
- When you have attention, use it fully — like a salesman who finally got into the busy man's office

### 8. Sampling & Risk Reversal
- Let the product sell itself: free sample, trial week, send on approval
- **Pepsodent:** film on teeth (tangible problem) + free tube → habit
- Buyer who **asks** for sample after reading story is in different psychology than recipient of unwanted gift
- Pay retail for trial sample > "free" — maintains value (Hopkins coupon test)

### 9. Psychology (Human Nature Is Fixed)
| Principle | Application |
|---|---|
| **Curiosity** | "Grains puffed 8×" / "foods shot from guns" — Puffed Wheat |
| **Cheapness fails** | Americans want value, not to feel poor |
| **Price signals quality** | "$100,000 for this formula" — specificity + prestige |
| **Personalization** | Name in gilt on books; limited-to-[group] offers |
| **Mental impression** | Point out qualities — people find what you direct them to see |
| **Reciprocity** | Free brush → obligation to reciprocate |
| **Comparison courage** | "Try our rivals too" — confidence signal (substitution cure) |

### 10. Test Everything
- *"Almost any question can be answered cheaply, quickly, and finally by a test campaign."*
- Split-run: same body, different headline — measure cost per customer
- 1% improvement matters at scale
- Your opinion < market's measured action

### 11. Mail-Order Lessons (Apply to All Ads)
| Lesson | Implication |
|---|---|
| Small type often outpulls large | Don't pay double for space without double returns |
| No wasted space | Every line earns its place |
| No palaver or boasting | Super-service only |
| Coupons/reminders | Reader forgets in 5 minutes — give action device |
| Pictures must sell | Or remove them — incubator photo +50% cost, zero sales |
| Complete story wins | Brief ads are never keyed |

### 12. Information Ads
- Offer wanted **information** — books, reports, guides
- Lead with education; selling follows naturally
- Free information builds authority (feeds Kern's Results in Advance + Hopkins service)

### 13. Individuality
- Laws are universal; **application** requires individuality
- Imitation is reproach — but fundamentals are fixed
- Test until you find *your* winning ad, then don't change it (Mead Cycle: not for $10,000)

### 14. Negative Advertising
- *"Watch out for substitutes"* — selfish, fails
- Courageous comparison, guarantees, specific superiority — wins

### 15. Letter Writing
- Personal letters outperform printed circulars
- Same A-Pile logic Halbert later codified

## Hopkins Campaign Case Studies

### Schlitz Beer — Preemptive Specificity
Every brewer purified water, cooled in filtered air, washed bottles. Hopkins **described the process first**. Schlitz rose from fifth to tied for first — not because they were unique, but because they **told the story first**.

**Architect move:** What does your industry do that no one says? Say it with specifics.

### Pepsodent — Problem + Sample + Habit
Hopkins found *"film on teeth"* — run tongue across teeth. Offered free tube. Created daily brushing habit. Market leader.

**Architect move:** Find the tangible micro-problem. Let them experience the fix.

### Quaker Oats / Puffed Cereals — Curiosity
"Shot from guns" / "8 times normal size" — curiosity drove trial.

### Shaving Soap — Specific Beat Generic
"Softens beard in one minute" / "130 formulas tested" — defeated all generic "abundant lather" claims.

## Hopkins Copy Checklist (run on every ad)

### Before writing
- [ ] Studied product like a salesman — house-to-house if needed
- [ ] Interviewed / researched typical buyer (VOC)
- [ ] Found the preemptive truth or specific mechanism
- [ ] Planned tracking (coupon, code, UTM, pixel)

### Headline
- [ ] Selects right audience (not clever-for-all)
- [ ] Self-interest obvious at a glance
- [ ] Hours of variants considered

### Body
- [ ] Salesman test passed on every line
- [ ] Reason-why for every claim
- [ ] Specifics replace all platitudes
- [ ] Full story for new prospects
- [ ] Service offer — sample, trial, information, approval
- [ ] No entertainment, no fine writing, no boasting

### Offer
- [ ] Risk on seller (trial, guarantee, sample on request)
- [ ] Product experience path clear

### After publish
- [ ] Keyed and measured
- [ ] One variable tested against control
- [ ] Winner kept; loser killed

|---|---|
| *"The only purpose of advertising is to make sales"* | Justifying direct CTA / cutting vanity metrics |

| *"Address the people you seek, and them only"* | Headline too broad |

| *"No argument compares with one dramatic demonstration"* | Adding proof, demo, sample |
