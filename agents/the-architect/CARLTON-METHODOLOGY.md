# John Carlton Methodology

Theatre, salesmanship, bonding, and the Simple Writing System for The Architect. **One voice** — Carlton supplies sales-detective research, hook engineering, incongruent juxtaposition, peer-to-peer bonding, and the 17-step assembly checklist. Not a separate mode.

**Core lineage:** Halbert protégé circle · Marketing Rebel · *Simple Writing System* (SWS)

**Sources:** User synthesis + Carlton SWS seminar notes. See `SOURCES-CARLTON.md`.

## Carlton in One Sentence

> **Advertising is salesmanship in print — 80% detective work on the street, 20% writing; then Theatre + Salesmanship + Bonding to hook, prove, and close like you're cracking a beer with one skeptical friend.**

## Philosophical Foundation

| Axiom | Carlton application |
|---|---|
| Not creative self-expression | Copy = **sales** — measurable conversion |
| Human-to-human connection | Bypass corporate fluff; intimate dialogue |
| Copy is assembled, not invented | Research first; hooks from real stories |
| 80/20 rule | **80%** market/product/competitive research · **20%** writing |
| Operation Money$uck | Every action must move prospect toward transaction |

**Architect rule:** If you can't close the prospect verbally, you can't close them in print. Find missing logical links before drafting.

## The Tripartite Persuasion Architecture

Every high-converting Carlton piece balances three pillars:

### 1. Theatre

Readers live in routine and distraction. Copy must **inject drama** into their day.

| Tool | Application |
|---|---|
| Single-line paragraph breaks | Eye relief; momentum |
| Dramatic subheads | Visual stops on the page |
| Power words | Shock, delight, edge |
| Mild shock / edginess | Pattern interrupt (not gratuitous) |
| Visual hierarchy | Headline + subhead splits |

**Purpose:** Prevent cognitive fatigue. Propel reader down the greased slide.

**Overlap:** Haines bucket brigade · Sugarman slippery slide · Collier word pictures

### 2. Salesmanship

The **logical spine** — face-to-face selling in print.

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| Product mastery | Know functionality cold |
| Competitive positioning | USP vs market |
| Objection handling | Every doubt addressed |
| Close verbally first | Test argument out loud |
| Don't sell from your heels | "I have what you need" — assume the sale |

**Hopkins alignment:** Offer service; give value; reciprocity kicks in.

### 3. Bonding

Empathetic alignment — peer, not pitch.

> *"Just you and me sitting down, cracking a beer, and shooting the shit."*

| Move | Detail |
|---|---|
| Inside language | Jargon/slang of the tribe — instant credibility |
| Avatar specificity | Not "parents" — "parent with three kids, one in jail, two in kindergarten" |
| Skeptical reader | He wants a reason NOT to read — give him none |
| "I'm just like you" | Disarm defenses; trusted recommendation tone |

**Overlap:** Kern conversational · Halbert Dear Friend · Collier mental conversation

### Operation Money$uck

High-ROI activities only. No vanity copy. Every paragraph earns toward the transaction.

## The Sales Detective Methodology

Corporate brochure copy fails. **Hooks live on the street.**

| Wrong source | Right source |
|---|---|
| Approved company literature | Inventor, field sales, customer service |
| Marketing department "company line" | Admin staff gossip, support tickets |
| Feature lists | Unvarnished anecdotes, why people *actually* buy |

**Carlton's move:** Interview the inventor or the salesman. Play dumb with competitors. Read Dale Carnegie for employee access.

**Architect:** Pair with `VOC-RESEARCH.md` + Kennedy 10 Questions + Collier conversation diagnostic.

### Hot Seat Head Grinder (must answer in few sentences)

- Who ARE you?
- What do you sell?
- Why are you in this biz?
- What do you do for customers? (Sell the **hole**, not the drill)

## Hook Engineering

A hook = **unexpected narrative twist** or **high-contrast angle** that interrupts pattern recognition and demands resolution.

### The One-Legged Golfer Benchmark

*"Remarkable Leather-Jacketed One-Legged Golfer Reveals His Incredible Secret to Hitting 280-Yard Drives"*

| Dynamic | Lesson |
|---|---|
| **Extreme contrast** | One leg vs 280 yards — cognitive dissonance |
| **Pattern interrupt** | If he can, excuses die |
| **Incomplete story** | Mechanism withheld — must read body |
| **Mechanism anchor** | "Triple Coil Swing" — torque, leverage, physics — reframes skepticism |

### How to find hooks

Interview for: gossip, myth, legend, competition, common wisdom, **incongruent juxtaposition**.

| Template | Structure |
|---|---|
| Weak beats strong | Skinny golfer, 425-yard drives |
| Accidental discovery | "Accidentally started hitting…" |
| Enemy inside | "Your brain is trying to kill your game" |
| Outcast triumph | Naked girls laughed… until knife fight |
| Crippled/outlier wins | Blind golfer, arthritic 55-year-old humiliates pros |

**Rule:** Hook must **immediately** transition to credibility — reader looks for reason not to believe.

## Story Architecture (Lean DR Narrative)

Three parts only: **setup → rising action → punchline.**

Every anecdote passes two filters:

### 1. "So What?" test (Frank Kern: "Who gives a shit?")

After every paragraph: *Why should the prospect care?* If it doesn't enhance life, save time, or kill pain — **cut it**.

### 2. "Why are you telling me this?"

Prospects distrust unprovoked stories. **Bridge explicitly** to reader's self-interest.

### Iconic situation (memorable visual)

- Hometown boy makes good
- Loser learns to win
- Clueless dude gets a clue
- Outsider sneaks inside
- Regular guy discovers exceptionality

**Voice:** Write in your own voice — don't imitate Carlton. Drop adjectives if stuck (Hemingway). Handwrite winning ads to internalize rhythm.

## The Greased Slide

Prospect is **Jabba on the couch** — welded, somnambulant. Getting wallet out is one of hardest human acts.

| Tool | Job |
|---|---|
| Theatre formatting | Visual momentum |
| Bucket brigade / seeds | Line earns next line |
| Turbulence (Step 15) | Can't sleep until resolved |
| Assume the sale | Close walks them through |

## Simple Writing System (SWS) — 17 Steps

Carlton's internal checklist before every project. See `research/CARLTON-SWS-TEMPLATE.md`.

| # | Step | One-line job |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Market Research** | Competition, market size, Hot Seat Grinder, prospect attitude |
| 2 | **Avatar** | Average prospect — demographics, pain, what she needs to hear |
| 3 | **USP** | Non-clever positioning; Id ticklers; inside language |
| 4 | **Features & Benefits** | Master list; "...that means..."; real emotional benefit |
| 5 | **Bullets** | One-two punch; blind fascinations; mini-stories |
| 6 | **Testimonials** | Word-for-word voices; specific results; photos |
| 7 | **Hooks** | Incongruent juxtaposition → credibility bridge |
| 8 | **Salesmanship Mentality** | Don't sell from heels; go-to guy; offer service |
| 9 | **Voice & Story** | Setup/plot/punchline; iconic situation; credibility |
| 10 | **Headlines** | One job: read first sentence; rips + originals |
| 11 | **Sales Funnel** | Campaign type, delivery system, offer architecture |
| 12 | **Simple Basics** | Who I am → What I have → Why now → What to do |
| 13 | **Huge Promise** | Truthful unbelievable opportunity; specific not general |
| 14 | **Reverse All Risk** | Guarantee placement; spouse/neighbor fear |
| 15 | **Turbulence** | Urgency, limitation, takeaway — off the fence |
| 16 | **Knock Off Fence** | Bonuses; better, more, bigger |
| 17 | **Close** | Walk through action; first person; assume sale |

**Checkpoint formula (Step 12):**

```
Here's who I am.
Here's what I've got for you.
Here's why it's important for your life right now.
Here's what to do now.
```

Not every asset needs all 17 — but **skipping a required element costs sales**.

## Bullets: Carlton One-Two Punch

| Punch | Job |
|---|---|
| **1** | Bold benefit / curiosity (positioning statement) |
| **2** | Parenthetical whisper — proof, exclusion, "99% ignore" |

**Blind bullets** — withhold mechanism; curiosity drives read (Halbert/Haines alignment).

**Name things** — "Lazy Businessman's 3-Step Shortcut" adds perceived value to short info.

## Headlines (Step 10)

> *"The headline has only one purpose — to get him to read the first sentence."*

| Principle | Detail |
|---|---|
| Rips (Caples/Schwab) | Model tested winners — learn originals too |
| Long vs short | Long when short cheats the sale; "I Quit" (Halbert) for short |
| Shock from zombie state | Be the one thing that wakes him today |
| Visual hierarchy | Headline + subhead when title runs long |

## Promise, Risk, Turbulence, Close

### Huge Promise (Step 13)

Truthful, comfortable, **specific** — not "be happier" but "be happier as a dad."

Magic vs reality: cold hard truth (eat less) doesn't sell; **mechanism + magic pill framing** does — anchor in physics/reason-why after hook.

### Reverse All Risk (Step 14)

Guarantee ≠ headline. Overcome: fear of being fooled, spouse, neighbors finding out.

### Turbulence (Step 15)

Not generic "only 15 left." Create **mental inability to sleep** — takeaway to guy below on list, market test, price may rise, site may come down.

### Knock Off Fence (Step 16)

Bonuses — sometimes order is **for one bonus alone**.

### Close (Step 17)

First person. Assume sale. Walk through wallet/PayPal. **Money shot is the close** — swipe closes, not just headlines.

## Carlton Lines (Operational)

| Line | Use when |
|---|---|
| *"80% research, 20% writing"* | Jumping to draft too fast |
| *"Sales detective, not company line"* | Corporate brochure tone |
| *"Cracking a beer, shooting the shit"* | Stiff, formal copy |
| *"Gun to the head — what survives?"* | Bloated, clever, weak hook |
| *"So what? / Who gives a shit?"* | Flabby paragraphs |
| *"Why are you telling me this?"* | Random story with no bridge |
| *"Incongruent juxtaposition"* | Boring, expected headline |
| *"Don't sell from your heels"* | Weak CTA, apologetic close |
| *"The money shot is the close"* | Strong headline, weak order form |
| *"Find the line, take 3 steps over"* | Safe, forgettable copy |

## Carlton Checklist (Gate Before Ship)

- [ ] Sales detective research done (not brochure copy)
- [ ] Avatar specific enough to recognize themselves
- [ ] USP clear, non-clever, targets average fox not dogs (Halbert)
- [ ] Hook = incongruent juxtaposition + credibility bridge
- [ ] Theatre: formatting creates eye relief and momentum
- [ ] Bonding: inside language, peer tone
- [ ] Salesmanship: every objection answered
- [ ] So What? passed on every paragraph
- [ ] Story bridges to reader self-interest
- [ ] SWS 12 basics stated plainly
- [ ] Turbulence + bonuses + first-person close
